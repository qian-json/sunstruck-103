Sunstruck 103°

A Houston heat-safety game

The power's out and it's 103° outside. Walk to the cooling center at the library before you overheat.

  • The sun heats you up. Asphalt heats you fastest and grass slowest. Shade cools you down, so walk from shadow to shadow.
  • You have a water bottle. Drink before you feel bad, and refill at fountains and water tables.
  • Your neighbors need help too. Talk to them, read their symptoms, and pick what to do.
  • Read the signs. They have real numbers about heat in Houston.

    What the signs said

      Achievements

        The two to know

        Heat exhaustion

        Heavy sweating. Cold, clammy skin. Dizzy, headache, nauseous, weak.

        Get somewhere cool, loosen clothes, sip water.

        Heat stroke

        Body temp 103°F or higher. Hot, red skin. Confused, slurring, or passing out.

        Call 911. Cool them down fast. No drinks.

        The name comes from 103°: the heat index where the National Weather Service calls heat dangerous, and the body temperature where heat stroke starts.
